Module 1

Textiles and the challenge

sortingTextile job sorter0/5

Learning job: Connect a textile's property to the job it must do for a user.

Sort each observation into the design question it best answers. Use the property named in the observation, not your favourite material.

1A fabric withstands repeated rubbing without wearing through quickly.

2A strap spreads the load comfortably across the user's shoulder.

3The carry item holds the user's library books without spilling them.

4The colours and surface design communicate the student's reuse message clearly.

5The handle length lets the intended user carry the bag without an awkward reach.

Need a nudge? Ask: What job is being tested, and which named property gives evidence about that job?

Safe textiles practice

scenario decisionStop, control, report0/5

Learning job: Apply a scan–assess–control–report routine to realistic textile-room hazards.

Choose the safest first response. Assume you must follow the teacher's local procedure and must not repair equipment yourself.

1You notice a damaged power lead before anyone uses the machine.

2A pin has fallen onto the floor near a walkway.

3Water has spilled near an electrical work area.

4A bag strap is stretched across the aisle.

5You are unsure whether the iron is cool enough to pack away.

Need a nudge? A strong first response stops exposure, protects other people and reports what needs teacher control.

Module 4

Stitch skills

sortingStitch job sorter0/5

Learning job: Distinguish joining, reinforcement, raw-edge finishing, decoration and testing jobs.

Sort each production intention by its main job. Actual stitch selection and settings must be tested and teacher-approved for the local machine and fabric.

1Connect two bag-body layers along the authorised seam line.

2Secure the beginning and end of a key seam using the authorised backstitch process.

3Control suitable raw edges before they are enclosed or handled repeatedly.

4Create a visible original stitched line whose main purpose is appearance.

5Sew on a matching offcut to see how the fabric, thread and stitch behave.

Need a nudge? Name the intended job first. A stitch name alone does not explain why it belongs.

Module 6

Responsible design

sortingLife-cycle impact map0/5

Learning job: Locate likely environmental impacts and realistic design responses across a textile product's life cycle.

Sort each statement into the life-cycle stage it most directly describes. Then identify the decision a designer can influence.

1A designer chooses a material that can meet the job without unnecessary waste.

2Pattern placement reduces avoidable offcuts.

3A strong, useful handle encourages the item to be chosen repeatedly.

4A small damaged area can be accessed and repaired.

5The owner decides whether parts can be reused, recycled through an available pathway or must be disposed of.

Need a nudge? Ask when the impact happens: while choosing materials, while making, during repeated use and care, or when the item reaches the end of its useful life.
