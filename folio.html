My folio

Keep the design thinking beside the making. Eight brief-led stages preserve selected project evidence without pretending to submit anything.

💾Browser-local workspace. Written evidence and selected images stay on this browser and device unless you deliberately back up, export or print them. Saving or exporting is never submission.

Evidence boundary

This folio keeps the strongest evidence of how your design developed, was made, tested and evaluated. Your writing saves in this browser. It is separate from theory quizzes and from any teacher-owned practical or assessment record.

  • Activities are formative practice. A student may transfer one relevant finding into the matching folio card, but activity scores and quiz responses are not copied automatically.
  • Student explanations and photos are not proof of competence or teacher observation.
  • The folio supports project evidence, but check current formal submission and marking requirements with your teacher.
  • Use only your own privacy-safe project evidence.

The JSON backup excludes project images. The read-only HTML export embeds only images the student deliberately added and browser-rasterises them to remove original metadata. Browser print/PDF may include images visible on this page. Creating any file does not submit it; check the current destination, naming rule and submission process with your teacher.

1. The need and design briefNot started

Action: Explain the environmental problem, intended user and reusable personal carry item you are being asked to develop.

Why it matters: A clear brief keeps every later decision connected to a real user and purpose.

Evidence to keep: A concise student-written need statement and design brief in your own words.

Sentence support

  • The intended user needs…
  • The carry item should…
  • This responds to the environmental problem by…

2. Criteria for successNot started

Action: Write a small set of observable working criteria linked to the brief and intended user.

Why it matters: Criteria guide design choices now and give you something fair to test later.

Evidence to keep: Student-written draft criteria covering relevant factors such as function, durability, aesthetics, ergonomics, ethics and environmental impact.

Sentence support

  • The carry item should… so that…
  • I could test this by…
  • This matters to the user because…

Related learning: m07-s1

Not started

3. Research and three ideasNot started

Action: Record useful research, its source and three genuinely different original design directions.

Why it matters: Research should expand your choices, while different ideas let you compare ways of meeting the brief.

Evidence to keep: Relevant research notes with source labels, plus three annotated student-created concept drawings or imported images.

Sentence support

  • This source helped me understand…
  • Idea 1 is different because…
  • I transformed the inspiration by…

4. Chosen idea and production planNot started

Action: Choose a design direction, justify it with evidence and build a teacher-confirmed production plan.

Why it matters: A justified plan turns the design decision into manageable, connected production actions.

Evidence to keep: Final annotated design, comparison and justification, confirmed pathway, resource list and ordered action plan with checkpoints.

Sentence support

  • I chose this idea because…
  • The evidence that influenced me was…
  • This step must happen before… because…

Related learning: m07-s3 · m08-s1 · m08-s2 · m09-s1

5. Safe making decisionNot started

Action: Explain one important safety or technical decision you applied during approved practical work.

Why it matters: A focused explanation shows how knowledge informed your making without pretending the website verified practical competence.

Evidence to keep: One student explanation linked to an approved process, with an optional project-only photo or sample image.

Sentence support

  • The factor I noticed was…
  • I followed the demonstrated process by…
  • This supported safety or quality because…

Optional project image

Show the project, setup or sample only; explain the decision shown and keep people, names and labels out of the image.

6. Build checkpointsNot started

Action: Choose up to three purposeful checkpoints that show progress, a problem or an approved change.

Why it matters: A few strong checkpoints explain the making journey better than many decorative photographs.

Evidence to keep: Up to three dated project-only images or text records, each with a meaningful action–observation–result caption.

Sentence support

  • Checkpoint 1: preparation or sample
  • Checkpoint 2: body construction or problem
  • Checkpoint 3: finishing, quality check or approved change

Optional project image

At this checkpoint I… I noticed… I then… The result was…

7. Test the finished solutionNot started

Action: Run relevant approved tests against your selected criteria and record the observations.

Why it matters: Testing replaces vague opinion with evidence about how the carry item performs for its user and purpose.

Evidence to keep: Criterion, approved test method, observation and cautious conclusion for each selected test, with optional project-only evidence.

Sentence support

  • The criterion was…
  • I tested it by…
  • I observed…
  • This means… A limitation is…

Related learning: m10-s1

Not started

8. Final evaluationNot started

Action: Judge how well the solution met the brief and criteria, then propose one realistic improvement.

Why it matters: A balanced evaluation explains achievement, limits and the next useful design decision.

Evidence to keep: A higher-order final response using test evidence, ethical and environmental reflection, and one feasible improvement.

Sentence support

  • The strongest evidence was…
  • The solution met this criterion because…
  • A limitation or trade-off was…
  • My next improvement would be… because…

Related learning: m10-s1 · m10-s2 · m10-s3
