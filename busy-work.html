Puzzles

Five purposeful textiles puzzles for revision, fast finishers or a relief lesson. They do not affect your course progress.

How to use this bank

Open one puzzle, make an attempt, use the feedback and stop. The whole bank stays closed so you are not hit with a wall of questions.

sequence puzzleTextile transformation trail0/6

Learning job: Retrieve the fibre–yarn–fabric chain and connect it to material testing.

Put the six clues in order, then use the first word of each correct card to explain the chain aloud.

1Fibres form the small starting material units.

2Yarns combine fibres into longer strands.

3Fabric structures arrange yarns or fibres into usable sheet material.

4Properties describe how the textile behaves.

5Tests gather relevant evidence.

6Selection matches the evidence to the intended job.

Need a nudge? Move from material scale to behaviour, testing and design choice.

scenario gridSafety signal challenge0/6

Learning job: Retrieve the stop–control–report routine across six practical-room situations.

For each signal, choose STOP, CONTROL or REPORT as the most immediate missing action.

1A student notices a damaged lead but keeps setting up.

2A pin is on the floor and people are approaching.

3The hazard area is controlled, but the teacher does not yet know about damaged equipment.

4Loose jewellery is near the machine operating zone.

5A student is unsure about an iron's condition and reaches for it.

6A near miss has ended with no injury and no current exposure.

Need a nudge? Identify what is missing right now: pause exposure, protect the immediate area or tell the person with authority.

clue retrievalMachine function mystery0/6

Learning job: Retrieve common machine functions without relying on one model's layout.

Solve each functional clue. Check the local machine with the teacher before using any control.

1I carry the upper thread through the fabric.

2I hold the lower thread supply.

3I help hold layers near the stitching point.

4We advance the fabric from below.

5I describe the route followed by the upper thread.

6I am the speed at which you can still see, guide and stop accurately.

Need a nudge? Think about the job named in each clue, not where the part sits on a remembered machine.

Write the six terms from the clues. The printable layout may cross the words at shared letters.

1The distance from the raw edge to the seam line.

2Reversing briefly at a seam end to reinforce it.

3A small trial made before final production.

4Unwanted spreading of colour beyond a stencil edge.

5A fabric layer attached as a decorative shape.

6A planned feature changed between otherwise comparable tests.

Need a nudge? Use the clue's job or visible evidence. Accents are accepted but not required for typing appliqué.

evidence sort mysteryDesign evidence detective0/6

Learning job: Retrieve which records belong in a brief-led folio and why.

Sort six mystery records into KEEP, PRACTISE or CHECK AUTHORITY, then reveal the explanation.

1Three genuinely different annotated ideas.

2A completed glossary retrieval sheet.

3An exact current due date copied from an old notice.

4A build photo with a caption explaining an approved change.

5A machine setting remembered from another model.

6A fair test tied to one criterion and a specific observation.

Need a nudge? Ask what the record proves, whether it is about this project and whether a teacher-controlled fact is involved.
